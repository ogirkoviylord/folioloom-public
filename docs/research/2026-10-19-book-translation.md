# A book needs more than a fluent draft

*Research update · 19 October 2026*

FolioLoom started with a personal problem: translating books with tools that did not quite fit the way I wanted to work. As the project grew toward a dedicated workbench, I wanted to look beyond my own experience.

This research draws on publicly available discussions, user reports and expert material. It is desk research, not findings from our own customer interviews or testimonials about FolioLoom.

## Three problems worth investigating

**Keeping earlier decisions within reach.** In a [literary translation discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/TranslationStudies/comments/pghaob/), a translator describes tracking invented words and recurring terms in Excel while translating in Word. Looking between files adds another task alongside the translation itself. This is a historical personal account, not evidence that all translators work this way or that current tools cannot help.

**Checking the brief, not just the fluency.** An [editor reports receiving a well-written translation that did not follow a glossary and style guide](https://www.reddit.com/r/TranslationStudies/comments/1sb65mf/). Fixing it would mean extra, unpaid work. The post establishes neither a book project nor AI authorship; its relevance is the difference between smooth prose and following agreed decisions.

**Checking the document after processing.** A [bilingual EPUB tool user reports original text missing from the final book](https://github.com/yihong0618/bilingual_book_maker/issues/442). A finished file does not, by itself, establish completeness. We have not reproduced this bug or established current-version behaviour, and one thread cannot tell us how common the problem is.

## What this means for FolioLoom

The approach we are developing connects **author context → terminology decisions → translation draft → review → export**. The aim is to keep decisions with the text and make the resulting document easier to inspect, rather than treating a fluent AI draft as a finished book.

Internal document-processing, translation and glossary-authoring foundations exist. The web Workbench is still in development and is not publicly available. Connecting approved terminology to translation and completing the integrated review workflow remain development work. We have not demonstrated improved literary quality, time savings or publication-ready output. Checking accuracy in an unfamiliar language still needs qualified bilingual judgment.

## Existing workflows matter too

The research also includes counterexamples. A [ProZ discussion](https://www.proz.com/forum/cat_tools_technical_help/364021-cat_tool_for_book_translation.html) includes a translator who reports using Smartcat for three books. An [expert explanation of paragraph segmentation](https://translationtribulations.substack.com/p/paragraph-segmentation-in-trados) describes configuration options in established tools.

FolioLoom has to justify its place alongside processes that already work. Historical prices and old bug reports are not evidence of today's product limitations.

## Sources, method and limits

The [website research page](https://folioloom.com/research/) provides ten selected sources with context and caveats. The [full register](evidence-register.csv) contains 57 source records: 53 considered in the underlying reports, two unverified leads and two additional adjacent technical cases. These are **not 57 independent customers or confirmed incidents**.

The underlying reports were prepared on 18 October 2026, with selected sources rechecked on 19 October. The register preserves original source titles and links, with English annotations covering evidence type, inclusion, verification depth and limits. It includes opinions, expert material, secondary reporting and adjacent engineering cases as well as personal accounts. Duplicate discussions and crossposts are not separate evidence.

This is a nonrepresentative selection of public material. Roles are mostly self-described, language and role coverage is uneven, and only selected sources were rechecked. Reported bugs are not independently verified incidents. Some sources are archived or unavailable; these limits remain visible in the register. PDF cases are adjacent engineering evidence, not an expansion of FolioLoom's TXT/DOCX/EPUB scope.

The research helps identify questions to test. It does not establish willingness to pay, market size, FolioLoom's effectiveness or a competitive advantage. Those questions need direct conversations and comparisons on real, authorised projects.
